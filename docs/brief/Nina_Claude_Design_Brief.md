## 1. PROJECT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Nina is an independent Spanish teacher in Georgia who teaches adults Spanish **from zero (A1)** in individual online lessons. What makes her different: she teaches through **stories, situations and recurring characters** (სიუჟეტები), with beautiful purpose-built visual materials, at a gradual step-by-step pace (**Poco a Poco**). The website must feel like **entering Nina's own illustrated Spanish world**, not like a language school, an LMS or a kids' app. Each student gets a **personal cabinet**: their lessons live there, and inside each lesson are the materials for it (cards, dialogues, vocabulary, audio, video, documents, interactive exercises, homework).

**The big idea:** this is NOT "Spanish lessons + PDFs."
It is **Nina + a structured Spanish-learning world + a personalized digital cabinet + reusable, story-based materials.**

### 4.2 Colors inside learning materials
Inside the cabinet, learning materials (cards, exercises) sit on a calm beige surface, keep the brand accents for headers/frames, and may use extra colors inside the content where needed for clarity.
- Exercises: beige background, printed fonts only, light thin outlines on word tiles.
- **Pronunciation materials:** lowercase letters inside **pale-green irregular, organic circles**. This treatment must stay visually distinctive.

### 4.3 Two visual tones (same brand, same world)
**A. „ცოცხალი" — Lively:** stronger contrast, deep navy/teal, burgundy, mustard, expressive brush accents, architectural/botanical details, movement. Use for hero, stories, CTAs.
**B. „მშვიდი" — Calm:** restrained contrast, more whitespace, muted sage, cream, soft teal, understated illustration, calm editorial composition. Use for explanations, pricing, FAQ and the whole student cabinet.

The landing page **alternates** Lively and Calm sections to create rhythm.

### 4.4 Typography (final)
The site is mainly in **Georgian**, so every text style needs a Georgian-capable font.

| Use | Latin / Spanish | Georgian |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | **Montserrat** 600–700 | **FiraGO** 600–700 |
| Body & UI | **Montserrat** 400–500 | **FiraGO** 400–500 |
| Handwritten accents (short Spanish words only: "¡Hola!", "Poco a poco", "Hoy", "¡Vamos!") | **Caveat** | — (never handwritten for Georgian) |

- FiraGO and Montserrat pair well visually; set Georgian first in the font stack for Georgian text.
- Clean, highly readable, adult. Build hierarchy with size, weight and color, not with many families.
- Handwritten font only for short decorative Spanish accents on the landing page. Never for body text, never inside exercises.
- Suggested scale (desktop): Display 64 / H1 48 / H2 36 / H3 24 / Body 18 / Small 14; generous line height for Georgian (1.6 for body).

### 4.5 Graphic language
- Warm paper/cream backgrounds with subtle paper texture
- Hand-drawn details: mustard brush-stroke underlines, open brush circles echoing the logo frame, small doodles (arrows, stars, check marks)
- Organic, imperfect shapes for image masks and section dividers
- Botanical and Mediterranean architectural accents (bougainvillea, terracotta pots, tiles, arches, lanterns), used sparingly
- Soft rounded corners (8–16px); not everything pill-shaped
- Icons: simple line icons with a slightly hand-drawn feel, consistent stroke

### 4.6 Design anti-patterns (must NOT look like)
Duolingo · a children's game · a generic LMS · a corporate SaaS dashboard · an online-school template · Canva template overload · a Spanish tourist site · a Spanish restaurant · stereotypical flamenco + red/yellow flag.
Also avoid: kawaii, hearts everywhere, excessive flowers, decorative clutter, overly rounded childish UI, giant exaggerated character illustrations, laptop imagery everywhere.

**Content principle:** never decorate for decoration's sake. Every illustration should establish context, explain meaning, create memory, tell a story, or make the student want to continue.

**North star:** *"This feels like Nina's own beautiful, intelligent, playful Spanish world."* It should look like a real independent teacher created her own beautiful learning universe.

## 5. TEACHING METHOD (content source for the landing page)

### 5.1 Core approach
- **Stories, situations and characters** (სიუჟეტები) are the key differentiator. Language is met in meaningful situations; vocabulary and grammar grow out of them.
- Not "here's a rule, memorize it" but "here's a situation: you meet the language, understand it, use it, and then notice the pattern."
- **Inductive lesson flow:** video/material first → student observes → grammar explanation. Repeats.
- **Building blocks:** each new "brick" rests on the previous one; a weak foundation collapses later. Hence **Poco a Poco**.
- **Culture-integrated:** original characters' stories, songs, dance and games (e.g. salsa while counting to 8, flamenco while counting to 12). "I teach the Spanish world."
- **Personalized:** vocabulary tailored to each student's goal (study, travel, relocation); follows conversation tangents and brings matching materials next time.
- **Adaptive start:** pace and intensity are set in a first conversation: why Spanish, available time, how the student thinks they learn best, preferences.

### 5.2 Nina's four classroom rules (strong landing-page content)
1. **We have fun.** No serious learning without enjoyment.
2. **We learn thoroughly**, at a comfortable pace, building solid foundations.
3. **We love mistakes.** Say whatever comes to mind; mistakes show what to focus on.
4. **We speak Spanish from lesson one.** Nina speaks Spanish phrases and translates so the ear adjusts.

### 5.3 Nina's story (for "Meet Nina")
- Has learned languages her whole life: besides Georgian, she studied German, Russian, English, Spanish and Italian.
- She wanted her own learning to be interesting, fun, varied and tailored to her goals, and that is exactly what she builds for her students.
- Do **not** mention her own current Spanish level on the website.

### 5.4 Example of the method: "Spanish in Action"
A topic about giving/understanding directions **during a dance class**.
Core words: derecha, izquierda, delante, detrás, arriba, abajo. Action words: gira, para, rápido, despacio.
Language is learned through a real action, not memorized lists. Good candidate for a small interactive teaser on the landing page.

## 10. STUDENT CABINET — DEVELOPER REFERENCE (MID FIDELITY)

**Purpose:** show the developer structure, components, states and flows. Brand-consistent (Calm tone, cream background, teal/navy UI), not marketing-level polish.

**Feeling:** "This is my Spanish journey." Not an admin dashboard, not a file folder.

**Core concept:** student logs in → sees **their lessons** → opens a lesson → sees **the materials for that lesson**. The student sees **only** what Nina has marked as ready for them. Accounts are created by Nina; there is no public sign-up.

### 10.1 Screens to produce
1. **Login:** email + password, "forgot password", warm illustration. No registration.
2. **Home — "ჩემი ესპანური" (My Spanish)**
   - Greeting: "¡Hola, [Name]!" + small Nina spot illustration
   - **Continue learning:** current lesson + next unfinished material + progress
   - **Homework** due
   - **From Nina:** teacher's notes and personal materials
   - Progress overview (e.g. a small Poco a Poco path)
3. **My lessons**
   - Lessons in order: number, date, title, topics covered, status (new / in progress / done), material count
   - Optional visual: Poco a Poco path with stops
4. **Lesson page (core screen)**
   - Title, date, short note from Nina
   - Topics covered in this lesson
   - **Materials list**, grouped or ordered as Nina arranged them; each item shows type icon, title, status (not started / opened / completed) and "mark as done"
   - Homework block
   - Continuous scroll, no pagination
5. **Material viewers** (show one frame for each):
   - Image card (9:16, zoom, swipe next/prev on mobile)
   - Video player (+ dialogue text below)
   - Audio player (+ transcript / dialogue ES + EN)
   - Document viewer (inline PDF preview + download)
   - Dialogue (Spanish + English, translation toggle)
   - Interactive exercise player (see 10.3)
6. **My materials (library):** everything assigned so far; filter by type and topic; search.
7. **Vocabulary:** personal glossary of words from covered topics; filter "up to topic N"; pronunciation treatment.
8. **Progress:** topics/lessons assigned vs completed, blocks overview, checkpoints.
Plus: **empty states** (e.g. "your first lesson with Nina is coming soon"), **loading**, **error**, and **mobile** versions of screens 2, 4 and 5. Mobile navigation: bottom tab bar.

### 10.2 Cabinet rules
- Background always cream/beige; no dark mode.
- Printed fonts only inside exercises; large and readable.
- Exercises: continuous scroll, unambiguous correct answers, "restart" returns to the start of the exercise. **No name entry**: the student is already logged in.
- No textbook branding anywhere.
- Simple navigation, 4–5 items max: Home · Lessons · Materials · Vocabulary · Progress.
- Visually simple even though the system underneath is complex.

### 10.3 Interactive exercise player (important for future-proofing)
Exercises are **created in the admin panel**, by Nina or by an **AI agent from Nina's prompts**, and rendered in the cabinet. They should look **varied in UX** (different interaction patterns) while staying **consistent in branding**.

Design one shared **exercise frame** (title, instruction, progress, check/next, restart, completion screen) and show several **interaction layouts** inside it:
- Multiple choice / choose the right answer
- Swipe true / false
- Drag-and-drop sorting into categories
- Sentence builder (word tiles)
- Fill in the blank
- Matching pairs (word ↔ image / word ↔ translation)
- Branching dialogue (choose a reply, story continues)
- Listening exercise (audio + question)
- Final/cumulative checkpoint with a result screen

The frame and components should be modular so new exercise formats can be added later without redesigning the cabinet.

## 11. ADMIN PANEL & PLATFORM ARCHITECTURE — NOT TO BE DESIGNED (context only)

The cabinet must reflect this model. No admin screens are needed from Claude Design.

### 11.1 Separation of concerns
- **Brand:** identity, visuals, tone, philosophy
- **Content:** reusable materials in a central library
- **Curriculum:** course → blocks → topics
- **Lessons:** sessions with a student, holding materials
- **Assignments:** what is given to which student
- **Student data:** progress, completion, personal materials
- **Presentation:** how the student experiences it
Content can evolve without rebuilding the site.

### 11.2 Core entities
- **Material** (master object, lives once in the library): type, title, files (image / document / video / audio) or interactive content, tags, linked topics
- **Exercise** (a material type): created manually or by the AI agent, stored as structured data + a chosen interaction template
- **Lesson** (lecture): title, date, note, topics covered, ordered list of materials
- **Topic** (~45) → **Block** (~10) → **Course** (A1; later Survival Spanish, Spanish in Action)
- **Student** (account created by Nina)
- **Assignment** (lesson or material ↔ student) with a **"ready for student"** toggle; only ready items show in the cabinet
- **Progress** per student per material (not started / opened / completed)
- **Personal material** (exists for one student only)
- **Lead** (booking form submission)

### 11.3 Reuse example
Library: Material A, B, C → Student 1 gets A, C · Student 2 gets A, B, C · Student 3 gets B, C.
Updating Material A in the library updates it for everyone who has it. No duplication.

### 11.4 Admin capabilities (reference)
- Object management of **lessons + materials** (create, edit, order, reuse, archive)
- Media library for **images, documents, videos, audio** (upload, preview, tag)
- **Exercise builder** (manual)
- Students, assignments, "ready for student" toggle, per-student teacher checklist, progress view
- Leads from the booking form

### 11.5 Planned AI features (architecture must allow them; not in this design)
- **AI agent with a chat interface for the admin.** Nina describes what she needs; the agent (via LLM APIs, possibly Claude Code) generates exercises and materials, choosing varied interaction templates so exercises feel different in UX while staying on-brand.
- **Social Studio agent:** generates social-media posts for the brand (adapted from an existing social-studio agent built for another project).
- Implication for the cabinet: exercises and materials arrive as structured data rendered by reusable templates (Section 10.3).

### 11.6 Tech context
- Frontend on **Vercel**, backend + file storage volume on **Railway**.
- Files handled: images, documents, videos, audio (MP3 and others).
